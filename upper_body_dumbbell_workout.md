# Upper Body Dumbbell Workout — Body Recomposition

**Level:** Beginner
**Frequency:** 3x/week (e.g., Mon / Wed / Fri)
**Equipment:** Dumbbells + adjustable bench
**Structure:** Rotate Day A → Day B → Day A → Day B (alternate each session)

---

## Day A

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat Dumbbell Bench Press | 3 x 10-12 | 60-90s | Bench flat, main chest builder |
| Incline Dumbbell Row (chest-supported) | 3 x 10-12 | 60-90s | Set bench to ~45°, lie face-down, row |
| Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press | 3 x 10-12 | 60-90s | Bench upright, back supported |
| Dumbbell Bicep Curl | 2 x 12-15 | 45-60s | Standing, controlled tempo |
| Overhead Dumbbell Triceps Extension | 2 x 12-15 | 45-60s | Seated or standing, one or two-hand |

## Day B

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incline Dumbbell Bench Press | 3 x 10-12 | 60-90s | Bench at 30-45°, hits upper chest |
| Single-Arm Dumbbell Row | 3 x 10-12 per side | 60-90s | Support on bench, focus on squeezing back |
| Dumbbell Lateral Raise | 3 x 12-15 | 45-60s | Light weight, controlled — this one burns fast |
| Hammer Curl | 2 x 12-15 | 45-60s | Targets forearm + bicep |
| Dumbbell Skull Crusher (on bench) | 2 x 12-15 | 45-60s | Lying flat, elbows tucked |

---

## Progression Guidelines

- **Weeks 1-2:** Focus on form, not weight. Use a weight where the last 2-3 reps of each set feel challenging but doable with good form.
- **Progressive overload:** Once you can hit the top of the rep range (e.g., 12 reps) with good form on all sets, increase weight slightly next session (even 1-2kg per dumbbell matters).
- **Track your lifts** — write down weight x reps each session so you know when to bump up.

## Notes

- **Rest days matter** — muscle grows during recovery, not just during the workout. Don't add a 4th day yet; let your body adapt first.
- **Protein target:** ~1.6-2.2g protein/kg bodyweight daily (total, not just breakfast) to support the muscle-building side of recomp.
- **First 2-3 weeks may feel "too easy"** — that's normal and intentional. Beginners often get real strength gains just from nervous system adaptation before adding real load.
- **Coverage gap:** This program does not include lower body, core, true vertical-pulling lat work (e.g., pull-ups/lat pulldowns), dedicated rear delt work, or trap work. Rows still hit the back and rear delts indirectly, but not as directly as isolation work would.
- **Timeline:** Visible changes typically take 8-12 weeks minimum to notice, with more meaningful transformation over 6+ months of consistent training and nutrition.

## Maintenance Mode (once at desired physique)

- Shift from progressive overload to maintaining current weight/intensity.
- Volume can often be reduced (e.g., cut sets by 1/3 to 1/2) while keeping the same weight, and muscle will be maintained.
- Keep protein intake consistent — this still matters for preventing muscle loss even at maintenance.
- Periodically check that strength isn't steadily declining; small week-to-week fluctuation is normal, but a consistent downward trend means it's time to add stimulus again.
