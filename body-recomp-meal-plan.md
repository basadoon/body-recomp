## The Seven Rules

Everything in this plan follows from these. If you remember nothing else, remember these.

1. **135g of protein, Monday to Saturday.** This is the number the whole plan is built around. It's a target you hit, not a floor with slack — a normal day lands at 136g. 135g is 1.85g per kg of bodyweight at 160 lbs, inside the 1.6–2.2 range the evidence supports for building muscle while losing fat. **Sunday is untracked past breakfast and the snack** and lands nearer 126g; see [Sunday](#sunday).
2. **Weigh all meat and fish COOKED.** Weigh rice cooked too. Getting this wrong is the one mistake that actually breaks the plan — see [Weighing](#weighing--read-this-once-properly).
3. **Every serving of the day's protein carries about 30g of protein.** That's what sizes every portion in the rotation.
4. **Four meals a day.** Breakfast, lunch, an afternoon snack, dinner. Nothing else between them. On Sunday only breakfast and the snack are tracked.
5. **Lunch and dinner are identical, Monday to Saturday.** Both come out of containers you filled on Sunday — twelve of them, two a day. Tuesday's are the only ones with no meat in them; tuna goes in at the meal.
6. **Vegetables count as zero protein.** They're in the plan for volume and fibre.
7. **The oil goes on at Sunday's cook** — 4g on the breakfast chicken, 64g on the lunch and dinner chicken, 18g on the dory. Only the breakfast pan's 2 tsp and Tuesday's 1 tsp of tuna oil go in fresh. With portions this size the oil carries a real share of your daily fat, so it isn't optional anywhere.

## How Every Day Reaches 135g

Everything except the day's meat is fixed, Monday to Saturday:

| Component | Cal | **Protein** | Fat | Carbs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Breakfast — 2 eggs, 15g cooked chicken, tomato, onion, 2 slices bread, 250ml non-fat milk, oil | 545 | **33** | 22 | 51.5 |
| Snack — 2 slices bread, 250g non-fat milk, 30g peanut spread | 418 | **23.5** | 13 | 52 |
| Meal base × 2 — rice 7.5g, beans 10g, vegetables 0g | 618 | **18** | 2 | 125 |
| **Fixed subtotal** | **1,581** | **74** | **37** | **228.5** |
| Protein of the day × 2 servings, plus its oil | ~470 | **~62** | ~23 | 0 |
| **Daily total, averaged Mon–Sat** | **2,050** | **136** | **60** | **228** |

**74g arrives before the rotation** — 54% of the day's protein, from bread, milk, eggs and beans alone. The two servings of the day's protein then add 60–62g on top.

**Strictly, each serving needs 30.3g.** The rotation sizes them at 30–31g, which lands a normal day at 136g rather than exactly 135. That 1g is the whole of your margin — this plan carries no real buffer beyond it, so a sloppy weighing costs you. Weigh properly and the target looks after itself.

**Bread and milk are doing the heavy lifting.** They appear twice a day and carry 33g between them, more than either serving of meat. Skip the snack and the day falls to 112g — the difference between hitting the target and missing it, from the one meal with nothing to cook.

**The carbs are already at the ceiling.** 228g leaves no room anywhere: the rice is at 150g and the bread appears twice. Anything you add has to come out of something else.

## Breakfast — Almusal

Two eggs scrambled with chopped chicken breast, plus tomato, onion, bread and non-fat milk. The same every single day.

| Food | Amount | Cal | P | F | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole eggs | 100g **out of the shell** (2 large) | 144 | 12 | 10 | 0.5 |
| Chicken breast, **cooked** | 15g | 25 | 4.5 | 0.5 | 0 |
| Tomato | 120g (1 medium) | 22 | 0 | 0 | 5 |
| Onion | 55g (½ medium) | 22 | 0 | 0 | 5 |
| Whole wheat bread | 65g (2 slices) | 160 | 8 | 2 | 28 |
| Non-fat milk | 250ml | 88 | 8.5 | 0 | 13 |
| Canola oil | 2 tsp in the pan (9g) + 0.5g on the chicken | 84 | 0 | 9.5 | 0 |
| **Total** | | **545** | **33** | **22** | **51.5** |

**Tomato and onion are logged at zero protein**, the same as every other vegetable in this plan. They do carry about 2g between them; it isn't counted, and that's a margin in your favour.

**The 15g of chicken is a garnish, not a protein source.** It carries 4.5g, comes off the same Sunday air fryer batch as everything else, and is there because chopped breast through scrambled eggs is worth more at the table than 4.5g suggests. Leave it out and the day lands at 131g — under target, but not broken.

**The oil is not optional.** Non-fat milk carries no fat and the chicken almost none, so breakfast without the pan oil falls to 12.5g of fat and takes the six-day average to 51g, well under target. **The 2 tsp is what keeps that table in band.** You cook the eggs in it anyway, so it costs no effort.

### Two ways to make it

**Ginisang manok at itlog** — the default, one pan, 2 tsp oil.
Heat the oil, sauté the onion until it softens, add the tomato and let it collapse and the liquid cook off. Add the chopped chicken and warm it through — it's already cooked, so this is thirty seconds, not browning. Push everything to one side of the pan, pour the beaten eggs into the empty side, let them set for a few seconds, then fold it all together. **All the daily totals in this plan assume this version.**

**Soft-boiled with raw ensalada** — the no-pan alternative, but read the caveat.
Dice the tomato and onion raw, salt them, let them sit while the eggs boil. Soft-boil the eggs, split them over the top so the yolk runs into the salad, scatter the cold chopped chicken over, eat with the bread and milk. **This version skips the pan oil, so it comes in 80 cal and 9g of fat lighter** — which takes the six-day average to 51g of fat. If you make it, add those 2 tsp to that day's lunch and dinner instead. Protein is identical either way.

**The spread carries 11g of the snack's 13g of fat.** That is planned for, but it means the snack is not a place to add anything else fatty.

## Lunch and Dinner — Tanghalian at Hapunan

**These two meals are identical**, every day of the week. Two containers a day, filled the same way, reheated twelve hours apart.

| Food | Amount | Cal | P | F | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protein of the day | see rotation — **~30g P** | — | — | — | — |
| Steamed white rice, **cooked** | 150g | 195 | 4 | 0.5 | 42 |
| Red kidney beans, **cooked** and drained — *alternates weekly, see below* | 60g | 74 | 5 | 0.5 | 13.5 |
| Vegetables, **cooked** | 200g | 40 | **0** | 0 | 7 |
| **Base, before protein and oil** | | **309** | **9** | **1** | **62.5** |

Oil isn't in the base — it belongs to the protein, and is listed per-day in the rotation table, because the amount differs by protein.

**The beans.** 60g drained at lunch and 60g at dinner — 720g across the week, which is **300g dry**, soaked overnight and boiled on Sunday. **Weigh them cooked and drained** — dry beans take on two and a half times their weight in the pot, and the numbers above are cooked weight. They go into the containers with everything else. **Every other week the beans are replaced by mixed vegetables** — see the cycle below.

**The rice.** 150g cooked is what ~54g dry becomes. Cooked white rice is 130 cal and 2.7g protein per 100g; dry is 365 cal and 7.1g per 100g. Never log a cooked weight against a dry figure. It stays at 150g on mixed-vegetable weeks too.

**150g of rice is a small mound, and the vegetables are what fill the container.** Keep the 200g of greens honest — they carry the volume the rice doesn't.

### The two-week bean and vegetable cycle

The bean-or-vegetable component in the base alternates week by week. **One week red kidney beans, the next week Jolly mixed vegetables** — green peas, corn and carrots — then back to beans. Nothing else in the plan changes: same protein rotation, same oil, same daily totals, same Sunday.

| Per serving | Bean week | Mixed-vegetable week |
|---|---|---|
| Bean or vegetable, **drained** | 60g kidney beans | **115g Jolly mixed vegetables** |
| Cal · P · F · C from it | 74 · 5 · 0.5 · 13.5 | 73 · 4.5 · 0 · 13.5 |
| Steamed white rice, **cooked** | 150g | 150g — unchanged |
| **Base total** | **309 cal · 9g P · 62.5g C** | **308 cal · 8.5g P · 62.5g C** |

**It's a straight drop-in — nothing else moves.** Jolly mixed vegetables carry 9g of protein and 27g of total carbohydrate per 230g can. At 115g that's 4.5g of protein and 13.5g of carbs, against the kidney beans' 5g and 13.5g: **the carbohydrate matches to the gram** and the protein is half a gram light. Calories land within one of each other. The rice stays at 150g, the rotation stays the same, and **calories, carbs and Sunday's budget hold across both weeks — protein is the only thing that moves.**

**One can a day, 115g at lunch and 115g at dinner.** That's 6 cans a week, and the 230g can divides into exactly one day's two containers with nothing left over.

> **The one real cost: vegetable weeks run 1g a day lower on protein.** Two servings at 4.5g instead of 5g takes a normal day from 136g down to 135g — still on target, but it spends the whole margin, and it puts Tuesday at 133g. On these weeks in particular, weigh the chicken properly.

**Why alternate at all.** Variety, mostly — and the mix brings vitamin A from the carrots, which nothing else in the plan supplies in quantity. What it does not bring is fibre: 115g of mixed vegetables carries roughly 2.5g against the beans' 4.5g, so vegetable weeks run about 4g a day lower on an already thin fibre total.

**Why vegetables are logged at zero protein.** Cabbage, kangkong and pechay do contain 1.5–2.5g per 200g cooked, but it's incomplete protein and poorly absorbed next to egg and meat protein, so none of it is counted here. The breakfast tomato and onion are logged at zero for the same reason. Your true intake therefore runs 6–8g a day above what these tables say, which is a margin in your favour.

### Vegetable options — per 200g cooked

| Vegetable | Cal | P | F | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cabbage | 50 | 0 | 0 | 11 |
| Kangkong | 40 | 0 | 0 | 6 |

All three are interchangeable — swap freely, the macro difference is negligible. The 40 cal and 7g of carbs in the base table are the average of the three.

## Weighing — Read This Once, Properly

**Weigh all meat and fish cooked**, off the pan, after any fat is drained. Rice too. These are the raw amounts those cooked weights come from, which is what you actually buy:

| Protein | Cooked per serving | ≈ Raw equivalent | What's lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast | 100g | ~180g | ~44% water |
| Chicken breast, breakfast | 15g | ~27g | ~44% water |
| Cream dory | 160g | ~270g | ~40% water, more if ice-glazed |
| Tuna, drained | 100g = ⅔ of a 155g can | n/a | already cooked; oil poured off |

**Why this matters more than anything else in the plan.** Cooked chicken breast is 31g protein per 100g; raw is 23.5g per 100g. Same piece of meat, different denominator. **Weigh 100g raw and log it against the cooked row and you get about 23.5g of protein instead of 31 — 15g off your day, four days a week.** At 135g with only 1g of headroom, that single mistake breaks the plan outright.

**Air-fried breast shrinks harder than the tables predict** — 2 kg raw comes back as 1,130g, not the 1,500g a 25% loss would suggest. That changes what you buy and nothing else: meat that dry is denser than the 31g per 100g logged here, so the servings run a gram or two in your favour. Rice works the opposite way and is also weighed cooked, because it *gains* water instead of losing it.

**In practice: weigh the batch once, cooked, then divide.** The Sunday batch goes on the scale whole and comes to about 905g. Set 105g aside and chop it for the week's breakfasts, then portion the remaining 800g into eight 100g meals. You never weigh a single serving, and a batch that came out light shows up before it's in the containers rather than after.

## Sunday

**Breakfast and the snack are the only tracked meals.** Lunch and dinner are both free — nothing to weigh, nothing to log. It's also the day you ride, and the day you cook the week.

**Ride, eat, then prep.** Post-ride is when your muscles are most glycogen-depleted and best able to absorb a large carb-heavy meal, and the kitchen is easier on a full stomach than on an empty one. The cook is about 90 minutes, most of it unattended.

**Sunday is bigger than the other days on purpose.** Your 34 km ride burns roughly 770 calories above resting, but that is already averaged into your maintenance figure, so it buys Sunday nothing on its own. What does is the six weekdays running at ~2,050, about 177 under maintenance each: that banks ~1,060, and Sunday spends ~36 of it going slightly over. The week still averages 2,080 against a maintenance of 2,227.

| | Cal | **Protein** |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 545 | 33 |
| Snack | 418 | 23.5 |
| **The two free meals have** | **~1,300 cal** | whatever they carry |

**~1,300 calories across two meals is tighter than it sounds, and it binds.** A 2-pc Chickenjoy with rice and a drink is about 900 on its own, which leaves 400 for the other meal. One restaurant meal and one modest one fits; two restaurant meals does not. Filipino portions pass 700 without effort, so pick the big meal deliberately rather than discovering it at dinner.

**Sunday lands short of 135g, and that's the trade.** Breakfast and the snack bring 56.5g; two ordinary meals out bring 60–80g between them, which puts the day near 126g. The week still averages 134g — 1.85g per kg, inside the range that matters — because the other six days are on target. Ordering something meat-led at both meals keeps it there; two dessert-led or meatless meals are what would actually cost you.

**Expect the scale up 2–4 lbs on Monday morning.** The ride empties your glycogen and it refills with roughly 3g of water per gram, on top of the day's sodium and carbs. It is water. It clears by Wednesday. Reacting to it is the easiest way to talk yourself out of a plan that is working.

## Where the Calorie Number Comes From

**2,227 a day is your estimated maintenance — what you burn. This plan feeds you ~2,080.** That deficit of ~150 is worth about 0.3 lb of fat a week, or 1.2 lb a month. It is deliberately shallow: at 1.85g of protein per kg with three lifting days, a gap this size takes fat off without taking muscle with it, and it's small enough to hold for months rather than weeks.

| | Cal/day |
|---|---|
| BMR — Mifflin–St Jeor at 72.6 kg, 175 cm, age 27 | 1,690 |
| Sedentary baseline — desk-bound, low step count | +350 |
| 3 lifting sessions/week, ~180 net each, averaged over 7 days | +77 |
| 1 ride/week, ~770 net, averaged over 7 days | +110 |
| **Maintenance** | **~2,227** |

Cross-checked against Katch–McArdle at ~16% body fat: 1,687. The two agree within 5 calories.

**The lifting sessions are worth less than they feel like.** Forty minutes of dumbbell work is about 4–5 METs, which is ~230 calories gross and **~180 net** of what you'd have burned sitting. Treadmill-style estimates and watch figures routinely double this. The number moves with the weights — recheck it when the dumbbells change.

**The ride, costed properly.** 34 km at 17 km/h is exactly 2 hours. Climbing 313 m takes 258 kJ, rolling resistance on knobby MTB tyres 336 kJ, air resistance 279 kJ — about 873 kJ of mechanical work, which at ~23% efficiency is ~905 calories gross and **~770 net** above what you'd have burned sitting. That averages 121 watts over the two hours, which is an honest endurance effort rather than a hard one. Use 770. Bike computers and Strava typically run 15–30% high on a mountain bike and usually report gross, so don't take their number.

**Max elevation doesn't enter the calculation.** 307 m is the highest point you reach; 313 m is how much you climbed in total, and only the total costs energy. At that altitude air density is within 3% of sea level, which if anything makes the pedalling marginally easier.

**Why it's a weekly average and not a daily one.** Energy balance settles over days and weeks; nothing resets at midnight. Six days at ~2,050 and one Sunday at ~2,265 net out to 2,080. That is what makes banking the weekdays' calories for Sunday's free meals legitimate rather than wishful.

**This estimate carries ±10%** — your true maintenance is somewhere in 2,004–2,450. A deficit of 150 sits well inside that band, which means **the scale is the only thing that settles whether this is working.** Weigh yourself at the same time each morning, average the week, and compare week to week. If four weeks pass with no downward trend, your maintenance is at the low end and the deficit isn't real yet.

**Expect it to drift.** As you add muscle expenditure creeps up; as you lose weight it creeps down. Recheck the calibration every two or three months.

**NEAT is your largest remaining lever.** With one ride a week and a desk job, walking 8,000–10,000 steps a day adds 120–180 cal/day and roughly doubles the deficit — without taking a single gram of food off the plate. It is the cheapest thing available to you here.
