# Body Recomp Meal Plan

**Stats:** 5'9" · 155 lbs · 3 lifting days + 2 cycling days per week

**Targets:** ~2,300 cal · 140g+ protein · 65–75g fat · ~270g carbs

Three meals plus a small merienda. Lunch and dinner are identical — cook one batch at lunch, refrigerate half for dinner.

**Oil rule: chicken breast and bangus only.** Those two are the only things on this plan lean enough to need it. Tuna, sardines and ground pork all bring their own fat and get none. See [Where the Oil Goes](#where-the-oil-goes) for the full schedule.

**Cooking method does not change macros.** Three eggs are 216 cal, 18g protein and 15g fat whether they're boiled, scrambled or folded into an omelette. Every number in this plan is unchanged by the switch — swap any day's method freely if you feel like it.

**The egg method follows the pan.** On tuna days there is no pan: the tuna is already cooked, it needs no oil, and frying it only makes it stringy. So there is nothing to fold eggs into — boil them instead, and the whole breakfast needs one pot of water. On chicken and pork days a pan is already hot with oil or rendered fat, so the eggs go straight in after the protein. Never two soft-boiled days in a row.

### What breakfast actually looks like now

Dropping the omelette means the tomato and onion are no longer carried inside the eggs. They become part of the dish beside them.

**Soft-boiled days — Mon, Thu, Sat (no cooking at all):**

Dice the tomato and onion raw and mix them through the drained tuna — that's ensaladang kamatis na may tuna, and it needs nothing else. Soft-boil the eggs, split them over the top so the yolk runs into it, and eat with the bread and milk. No pan, no oil, no washing up beyond a pot. This is the easiest breakfast of the week and the raw tomato keeps its vitamin C, which the omelette version cooked off.

**Scrambled days — Tue, Fri, Sun (chicken) and Wed (pork):**

Ginisa the onion and tomato with the protein first — 1 tsp oil for the chicken, the pork's own rendered fat on Wednesday. Once the tomato has collapsed and the liquid has cooked off, push it to one side of the pan, pour the beaten eggs into the empty side, let them set for a few seconds, then fold everything together. Bread and milk alongside.

### Technique

**Soft-boiling.** Lower room-temperature eggs into water that's already at a boil. **6 minutes** for a runny yolk, **7** for jammy and easier to handle. Straight into cold water for a minute after — it stops the cooking and makes them peel cleanly. Add a minute if the eggs come out of the fridge cold.

**Scrambling.** Low heat, and pull them off while they still look slightly underdone — they keep cooking in the pan for another 20 seconds. **Beat a splash of your 250ml milk into the eggs** (about a tablespoon) before they go in; it makes them creamier and costs nothing, because that milk is already counted in the base above.

**Raw yolks and salmonella — worth knowing, not worth panicking about.** A 6-minute yolk is still runny, and you're eating three eggs a day, every day, so this is repeated exposure rather than a one-off. Buy from a stall with turnover, refrigerate them at home, and discard any with a cracked or dirty shell. If a batch ever gives you doubt, go **8–9 minutes** for a fully set yolk — you lose nothing nutritionally, only the texture.

**Buy tuna in water or brine, not oil.** Slice weight varies by brand — check your loaf's label and weigh two slices once. Milk stays in ml because the carton is marked that way (250ml ≈ 258g).

**Weigh meat and fish RAW, before cooking.** Meat loses ~25% of its weight as water, so 200g raw becomes ~150g cooked — the macros above belong to the raw weight. Chicken breast is 22.5g protein per 100g raw but 31g per 100g cooked; both describe the same piece. Weighing 200g *cooked* would be ~267g raw, a third more food than the plan intends. (Rice is the opposite — weigh that one cooked, since it *gains* water.)

**Wednesday: pour off the rendered fat.** Ordinary giniling is ~21% fat — 150g raw carries 32g of it, against 11g for the kasim it replaces. Brown it, tip the pan, and spoon out the pooled grease before the vegetables go in; that removes roughly 8g of fat and 70 cal per serving, and the 322/25/24 row above assumes you did. Skip the draining and Wednesday's serving is **394 cal and 32g fat**, putting the day past 2,580 cal and 109g fat.

**If your palengke will grind to order, ask for *giniling na kasim* — lean grind, no fatback.** It's the same cut the plan used before, just ground. At ~10% fat, 150g raw runs 240 cal / 30g protein / 13g fat, takes 1 tsp of oil back, and lands Wednesday at **~2,365 cal · 148g protein · 80g fat** — much closer to the rest of the week. Worth asking for; supermarket tray giniling almost never is.

Breakfast protein is cooked fresh each morning and is *not* part of these batches — except Wednesday, where it's easiest to brown the full 375g at once, take 75g for breakfast, and leave 300g for lunch and dinner.

**Tuesday is the no-cook day.** Three cans of sardines, split 1.5 per meal, eaten with the sauce — the can supplies the fat, so no oil. **Eat the bones.** They're soft enough to crush with a fork and they're the reason sardines are worth using: 1.5 cans is ~560mg calcium, about two glasses of milk. Tuesday covers your sardine quota by itself. **Canned tuna does not do this** — it's boneless, so the tuna breakfasts add protein but no calcium.

**Check your can's label.** These numbers assume 134 cal, 14.5g protein, 7.2g fat and 2.6g carbs per 100g of sardine can contents, and 116 cal / 25.5g protein / 1g fat per 100g of drained water-packed tuna. Brands differ a lot in how much is fish versus sauce — a saucier can may run closer to 12g protein per 100g, which would cost Tuesday ~10g of protein. Sodium also runs 400–500mg a can, so expect the scale up a pound Wednesday morning. That's water, not fat.

## Where the Oil Goes

**Chicken breast and bangus only.** Everything else on the plan carries enough fat to cook itself.

| Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner | Day total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | none (soft-boiled + tuna) | 2 tsp (chicken) | 2 tsp (chicken) | 4 tsp |
| Tue | 1 tsp (chicken) | none (sardines) | none (sardines) | 1 tsp |
| Wed | none (pork) | none (pork) | none (pork) | none |
| Thu | none (soft-boiled + tuna) | 2 tsp (chicken) | 2 tsp (chicken) | 4 tsp |
| Fri | 1 tsp (chicken) | 1 tsp (bangus) | 1 tsp (bangus) | 3 tsp |
| Sat | none (soft-boiled + tuna) | 2 tsp (chicken) | 2 tsp (chicken) | 4 tsp |
| Sun | 1 tsp (chicken) | free | 2 tsp (chicken) | 3 tsp |

**Why chicken breast gets the most.** It's 4g of fat per 150g serving with nothing protecting it — the leanest thing you eat all week. Without oil it sticks, then dries out before the centre is safe. The 2 tsp is doing real work.

**Why bangus still gets 1 tsp and not 2.** It brings 10g of its own fat per serving and the skin renders more once it's hot. One teaspoon to start the pan, then it largely fries itself. Skin-side down over medium heat.

**Why the soft-boiled days get none.** Boiled eggs need no fat, and the tuna is already cooked in the can. There is nothing in that breakfast for oil to do.

**Why ground pork gets none.** It renders ~32g of fat per 150g serving. The problem there is removing fat, not adding it.

That's roughly **85g of oil a week**, so a 1L bottle now lasts about two and a half months instead of one.

## Sunday — Cheat Day

One free meal at lunch, other meals normal. Anything you want, no tracking. That's about a 700–900 calorie surplus for the day, which spread across the week is ~120 extra calories daily — negligible.

If you'd rather have a full free day, bank for it: pull **80g of cooked rice from each meal** on the six other days — twelve portions at ~104 cal, ~1,250 calories saved to spend.

Expect the scale up 2–4 lbs the next morning. That's water and glycogen from the sodium and carbs, not fat. It clears in two or three days. Don't react to it.

## Notes

**Egg freshness matters more now.** Very fresh eggs are harder to peel — the white clings to the shell — so eggs a few days old actually soft-boil better than same-day ones. Cracking them into scrambled eggs one at a time into a separate bowl first is worth the extra step: one bad egg won't ruin the other two.

**Milk.** Buy full cream, not "filled milk" or "milk drink." Powdered full cream is ~40% cheaper than boxed UHT with the same calcium if you want to save.

**Calcium — the one gap left by dropping dilis.** A normal day now adds up to roughly 690mg: milk 300, pechay or kangkong ~150, beans ~84, eggs 78, bread 50, the rest incidental. That's about 70% of the 1,000mg you want. Neither chicken nor canned tuna brings meaningful calcium.

Tuesday is fine — the sardines' soft bones bring ~560mg, putting that day past 1,250mg. The other six days are short. Cheapest fix is a **second 250ml milk serving** (+300mg, ~990mg total) for about ₱30 a day; it also adds 150 cal, 8g protein, 8g fat. Alternatively run sardines two days a week instead of one, or favour pechay and kangkong over cabbage, which carries roughly a third of their calcium. Or accept ~690mg — it's a shortfall, not a deficiency, and worth knowing rather than assuming it's covered.

**Food safety.** Refrigerate the dinner portion within an hour of cooking, not after it cools on the counter. Cooked rice left at room temperature grows *Bacillus cereus*, and reheating does not destroy its toxin.

**Ground pork spoils faster than whole cuts.** Grinding exposes far more surface area, so buy it the morning you cook it or freeze it the day you buy it. It should be pink-red and smell of nothing; grey or sour means throw it out. Cook it through — no pink centres, unlike a steak.

**Canned beans are safe as-is** — already fully cooked, so rinse, drain, and eat. Refrigerate an opened can's leftovers in a container rather than in the tin, and use within 3 days. Same for tuna: never store an opened can in the tin.

**If you ever switch to dry kidney beans to save money, they must be boiled hard.** Soak 8+ hours, discard the soaking water, then a full rolling boil for at least 10 minutes before simmering to done. Raw and undercooked red kidney beans contain phytohaemagglutinin — severe vomiting and cramps within hours, from as few as four or five beans. Beans held at low temperature without that hard boil are *more* toxic than raw, so never use a slow cooker or rice cooker for them.

**Fiber.** The beans add ~18g of fiber a day on top of what you already eat. That's good for you but a big jump — start with 60g per meal for the first week and work up, or you'll spend a few days bloated and gassy. Drink more water alongside it.

**Vegetables.** Undercook slightly — they soften again on reheating. Pull kangkong and pechay off the heat while still bright green. Cabbage and bean sprouts hold up better for a reheated portion. Bean sprouts spoil in about 2 days in this climate, so buy them the morning you cook them.

**Whole wheat bread.** Many loaves sold as "whole wheat" here are refined flour with molasses for color. Check that whole wheat flour is the first ingredient.

**Saving money.** Buy fish and vegetables at the palengke early morning — usually 30–40% cheaper than supermarket. Whole chicken beats breast per kilo if you'll portion it yourself, and chicken is now your largest single line item. Eggs are the cheapest complete protein available; if a week gets tight, go to 5 eggs a day and skip the breakfast meat entirely.

**Prices** vary between palengke and supermarket, and between Manila and the provinces. Treat them as rough estimates.

## Tracking

Weigh yourself the same time each morning and only look at the seven-day average. Day-to-day swings of 2–4 lbs from water and food volume mean nothing.

If your average weight is stable and your lifts are climbing, the plan is working. If weight drops more than half a pound a week, add 150 calories. If it climbs steadily, cut 150.

Take progress photos monthly. During a recomp the scale barely moves, so photos and your training log are the real measures.
